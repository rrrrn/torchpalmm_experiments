\caption{Smoothing accuracy, parameter recovery and wall time (mean, SD in parentheses, over the $n$ replicates), and convergence, at $N=1000$. Within each ($D$, $s_n$) cell, accuracy and time are computed over the $n$ replicates on which python and the participating baselines all converged, so those rows describe identical datasets; a baseline that never converged in a cell is listed at $n=0$ and does not restrict the others. Errors are in their own units rather than as ratios to python, since a ratio of two near-zero or two equally poor errors is not interpretable. MISE is the median per-component ISE. The convergence column is the exception to the matching, giving each method's converged/attempted rate over \emph{all} replicates of the cell. Largest gap between a baseline mean and python's: beta 0.0015, sigma2 0.29, mise 0.01, D 0.13. Bold on an accuracy column marks the method whose mean is both significantly lower than every other method's in that cell (paired $t$-test on replicate-matched errors, $p<0.05$) and ahead of it by more than 10\%, so a floating-point-level but consistently-signed gap cannot bold on significance alone; bold on time or convergence marks a value beating the runner-up by more than 10\%. \texttt{bam}'s $D$ MAE is marked -- : its per-column variance estimate ($D_c=\hat\sigma^2/\lambda_c$, read off \texttt{mgcv}'s per-term smoothing parameters) is not comparable, since jointly fitting more than one random-slope term under the same grouping factor systematically under-penalizes every column after the first, inflating its estimate by roughly an order of magnitude in every replicate tested regardless of the underlying data.}
